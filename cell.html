Cells: the units of life

Cell theory states that all organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of structure and function, and cells arise only from pre-existing cells. Prokaryotes (bacteria and archaea, typically 1–5 µm) have no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; their DNA lies in a nucleoid and they have 70S ribosomes. Eukaryotes (typically 10–100 µm) compartmentalise their chemistry in membrane-bound organelles. Mitochondria and chloroplasts are descendants of engulfed bacteria (endosymbiosis): they have two membranes, circular DNA and 70S ribosomes.

Compartments let incompatible reactions run side by side: acid hydrolases work at pH ≈ 4.5–5 inside lysosomes while the cytosol stays near pH 7.2; the ER lumen is oxidising so disulfide bonds can form; the mitochondrial inner membrane holds a proton gradient.

The plasma membrane: fluid mosaic

A 7–8 nm phospholipid bilayer with hydrophilic heads facing water and hydrophobic tails inside, studded with proteins (about 50 % of membrane mass), cholesterol (which buffers fluidity) and glycolipids/glycoproteins forming the glycocalyx. The hydrophobic core is what makes the membrane selectively permeable.

Transport across membranes

Simple diffusion of small, non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂, N₂, steroid hormones) follows Fick's law: the flux is proportional to the concentration difference.

$$J = -D\frac{dC}{dx} \quad\Rightarrow\quad J = P\,(C_\text{out} - C_\text{in})$$

$P$ is the permeability coefficient (m/s). Facilitated diffusion uses channels (fast, selective pores such as K⁺ channels and aquaporins) or carriers that change shape (such as GLUT1 for glucose). Carriers saturate, following Michaelis–Menten kinetics:

$$J = \frac{J_\text{max}\,[S]}{K_m + [S]}$$

For GLUT1, $K_m \approx 1$–3 mM. Both forms of diffusion are passive and run down the electrochemical gradient. Active transport uses energy to move substances against their gradient. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (primary active transport) hydrolyses one ATP to export 3 Na⁺ and import 2 K⁺; because it moves one net positive charge out per cycle it is electrogenic. It uses about 20–25 % of the body's resting ATP (up to two-thirds in neurons). Secondary active transporters such as SGLT1 use the Na⁺ gradient it creates to import glucose.

Osmosis and water potential

Osmosis is the net diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential (lower solute concentration) to lower water potential. Solutes lower water potential by the osmotic (solute) potential, given by the van 't Hoff relation, and pressure adds to it:

$$\Psi = \Psi_s + \Psi_p, \qquad \Psi_s = -iCRT$$

where $C$ is molar concentration and $i$ the number of particles per formula unit ($i \approx 2$ for NaCl). Body fluids are about 290–300 mOsm/L; 0.9 % NaCl (154 mM, ≈ 308 mOsm/L ideally) is isotonic. Red cells behave as osmometers following the Boyle–van 't Hoff law $V = b + (V_0 - b)\,C_0/C$, where $b$ (≈ 40 % of $V_0$) is the osmotically inactive volume. They lyse when swollen beyond about 1.6 times their normal volume (≈ 150 mOsm/L, 0.45 % NaCl).

Plant cells have a rigid cellulose wall. In a hypotonic solution water enters until wall pressure ($\Psi_p$, turgor) balances the solute potential: the cell is turgid, which keeps non-woody plants upright. In a hypertonic solution the protoplast loses water and pulls away from the wall: plasmolysis. Wilting is the loss of turgor.

The endomembrane system and protein targeting

Proteins made on free ribosomes stay in the cytosol or go to the nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts or peroxisomes. Proteins with an N-terminal signal peptide are recognised by the signal recognition particle (SRP) and translated into the rough ER. From there COPII vesicles carry them to the Golgi, which modifies glycans and sorts cargo at the trans-Golgi network: secretory granules, plasma membrane, or (with a mannose-6-phosphate tag) lysosomes. Fusion of vesicles with target membranes is driven by SNARE proteins.

1. Sizes and magnification

A mitochondrion 1.5 µm long appears 30 mm long in an electron micrograph. Magnification $M = \text{image}/\text{actual} = 30\ \text{mm} / 1.5\ \mu\text{m} = 30\,000\ \mu\text{m}/1.5\ \mu\text{m} = 20\,000\times$.

The same micrograph shows a ribosome as a 0.5 mm dot: actual size $= 0.5\ \text{mm}/20\,000 = 25$ nm.

2. Surface area to volume

A spherical cell of radius 10 µm: $A = 4\pi r^2 = 1257\ \mu\text{m}^2$, $V = \tfrac43\pi r^3 = 4189\ \mu\text{m}^3$, $A/V = 3/r = 0.30\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$.

Double the radius to 20 µm: $A/V = 0.15\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$. Halving the surface available per unit volume limits how large a cell can grow and still exchange enough O₂ and nutrients by diffusion. This is one reason cells divide, and why absorptive cells carry microvilli.

3. Isotonic saline

0.9 % NaCl = 9 g/L. Molar mass 58.44 g/mol, so $C = 9/58.44 = 0.154$ mol/L. NaCl dissociates into 2 ions: ideal osmolarity $= 2 \times 154 = 308$ mOsm/L (about 286 measured, because the ions interact). Plasma is ≈ 290 mOsm/L, so 0.9 % saline is isotonic.

Solute potential at 25 °C: $\Psi_s = -CRT = -(300\ \text{mol m}^{-3})(8.314)(298) = -7.43\times10^{5}$ Pa $= -0.74$ MPa.

4. Red cell volume in a hypotonic solution

Boyle–van 't Hoff with $b = 0.4V_0$, $C_0 = 300$ mOsm/L. At 200 mOsm/L: $V/V_0 = 0.4 + 0.6 \times 300/200 = 1.30$: the cell swells by 30 %.

Lysis at $V/V_0 = 1.6$: $0.4 + 0.6 \times 300/C = 1.6 \Rightarrow C = 150$ mOsm/L (≈ 0.45 % NaCl). Try these values in the osmosis lab.

5. Plant cell turgor

Cell sap 400 mOsm/L: $\Psi_s = -0.4 \times 8.314 \times 298 / 1000 = -0.99$ MPa. In pure water ($\Psi = 0$) at equilibrium $\Psi_p = +0.99$ MPa, about ten times atmospheric pressure, held by the wall.

In 600 mOsm/L sucrose ($\Psi = -1.49$ MPa) the cell loses water until its sap reaches 600 mOsm/L: protoplast volume $\approx 400/600 = 67$ % and the cell is plasmolysed.

6. Fick's law

O₂ permeability of a lipid bilayer is roughly $P \approx 0.1$ m/s (very high). With 0.1 mol/m³ outside and 0.02 mol/m³ inside: $J = 0.1 \times 0.08 = 8\times10^{-3}$ mol m⁻² s⁻¹. Glucose's permeability is about $10^{-9}$ m/s, eight orders of magnitude less, which is why cells need GLUT carriers.
